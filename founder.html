Built by someone who spent fifteen years assembling these reports by hand.

Corporate finance and FP&A across Apollo, Garware and Ashok Leyland. Enough month-ends to know exactly where the fortnight goes, and enough diligence processes to know what an investor opens first.

FinLytTech exists because the same workbook kept getting rebuilt, in a different company, in a different city, every single month.

Why a finance person built this and not an engineer

Most reporting software is designed by people who have never had to defend a variance to a board. That shows in what it chooses to make easy.

The hierarchy is not negotiable

Schedule III has a shape, and a P&L that renders in a different order is not a styling preference, it is wrong. That conviction is built into the data model rather than bolted on as a report template.

Indian defaults, not translated ones

Party versus expense, inter-state treatment for a foreign vendor, the voucher state machine. These are the details that make imported software feel almost right and never quite usable here.

The number has to survive scrutiny

Every figure on every dashboard traces back to the voucher that produced it. If a director asks where a number came from, the answer is one click, not one week.

The company had the data the whole time. What it never had was fifteen spare days a month to turn that data into a decision.
