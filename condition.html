Can your business say where the cash went?

Most Indian businesses keep good books and still cannot answer five plain questions on the day they matter. The answers exist inside the ledger. Getting them out means a monthly MIS pack your team rebuilds by hand.

The books do not agree
Who owes us, and who do we owe? It depends which file you open.

Tally in one place, bank statements in another, vendor bills on a WhatsApp thread. Receivables and payables get stitched together by hand, and the person stitching is usually the person you hired to think.

The pack is rebuilt by hand
Where did the cash go? The answer waits for the monthly MIS pack.

Every month the same workbook is exported, pasted and rebuilt. By the time it is read, the month it describes has already moved on.

Nothing past today
How long do we last, and what is due this week? Nobody can say with confidence.

The books record what happened. Statutory dues, vendor payments and collections still land as surprises, and working capital drifts until it becomes a crisis to manage instead of a decision to make.

Why has it stayed this way?

Nobody chose this. It is what happens when a market gets excellent bookkeeping software and almost no layer above it that reads the books and says what to do.

Tally solved recording, and reading was left to Excel

It is genuinely good at what it does, and a great many Indian businesses run on it for that reason. A voucher store was never meant to answer "how long does our cash last", and asking it to has always meant exporting to a spreadsheet.

Large ERP answers it, after a long implementation

SAP and NetSuite can answer these questions well. The implementation runs for months, and most businesses cannot justify that before they know what the answers are worth.

GST quietly created the raw material

Invoices, returns and ledgers now exist as structured, dated data. Most of it is used to file the return and very little else.

So the pack got built by hand

Skilled accountants, in-house and at the CA firm, start every month rebuilding the same workbook, because no software sat between the books and the decision. Their judgement is the valuable part. The copy-paste is the waste.
